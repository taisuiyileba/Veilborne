## Moon-husk bloom sky (月骸花潮)

[moon_sky.png](../../src/main/resources/assets/veilborne/textures/environment/moon_sky.png) is now generated from the [painted source](../../tools/art/source/moon_sky_painting.png). The source was made with the built-in image_gen tool on 2026-09-26, following the sky panorama prompt below; no external reference image was used. The original generated file is `C:/Users/suiyi/.codex/generated_images/01a0db93-4e3b-7db1-ae1a-20bf90dd08e6/exec-138c6bfa-d8ed-462e-980e-8925b5e6b72c.png`. `tools/art/moon.py` retains its procedural sky as a fallback when the painted source is absent.

To install a painting, run `python tools/generate_assets.py --no-sound --moon-sky path/to/painting.png`. The file is copied to `tools/art/source/moon_sky_painting.png` and converted on this and every later run:

- It is resized to 2048 + 256 px wide and 1024 tall, and its two ends overlap. The last 256 columns cross-fade into the first 256: broad colour across the whole overlap, fine detail only across a 64 px band in its middle. The 2048 px result wraps without a step, a ghost or a mirrored stretch.
- Above about 56° the panorama cross-fades into a cap painted from a flat patch of the painting's own upper sky. The patch is laid onto the zenith like a photograph, with its lower edge toward the moon, and the blend is complete by 74°. A generated image is not drawn with an equirectangular projection's polar stretch, so without the cap its top rows would pinch into spokes around one point. A slight circular blur softens what the cap does not cover.
- Alpha is taken from brightness, so the dome's gradient and stars show through the darkest gaps.

A replacement painting therefore does not need a perfect wrap or a perfect zenith. It only needs the layout above and a calm, starry upper sky.

Layout the game expects:

- Upper-hemisphere equirectangular. The top edge is the zenith and the bottom edge is the horizon.
- The texture is rotated so that u = 0.5 always faces the moon. The moon hangs about 30° up, which is about two-thirds of the way down the image.
- The moon itself is drawn in game and must not be painted.

## Mirror-sky distance (镜天巡礼)

Beyond that radius the mirror shows a painted distance, [mirror_horizon.png](../../src/main/resources/assets/veilborne/textures/environment/mirror_horizon.png). The same painting wraps the gallery's windows and stands in for the sky inside the three standing mirrors.

Layout the game expects:

- Upper-hemisphere equirectangular, 2:1. The top edge is the zenith and the bottom edge is the horizon.
- Only the lower third or so is normally seen: nearer land is covered by the real reflection. Keep the detail there and keep the upper half calm.
- The game tints it: by day it is shown nearly as painted, and at night it is darkened to a deep lapis. Paint it as a daytime, low-contrast, silvery image.
- In a mirror overhead, distant land hangs upside down: bases toward the zenith, peaks pointing down at the horizon.

## Scarlet-seam cloth (绯线缝世)

The sky becomes cloth: [thread_sky.png](../../src/main/resources/assets/veilborne/textures/environment/thread_sky.png) is the silk the sky shader lays over the dome. It is now generated from the [painted source](../../tools/art/source/thread_sky_painting.png). The painting was made with an image generation model on 2026-09-27 from the prompt below, and supplied by the user as a 1568×784 JPEG. It was converted losslessly to PNG and installed through `--thread-sky`; no external reference image is known to have been used. Its two ends did not meet exactly (the raw step across the join was about four times an ordinary column-to-column step); after the converter's cross-fade the step equals an ordinary one. `sky()` in `tools/art/thread.py` remains as the procedural fallback when the source file is absent: two layers of meandering silk folds hanging from a dark crown.

To install another painting, run `python tools/generate_assets.py --no-sound --thread-sky path/to/painting.png`. The file is copied to `tools/art/source/thread_sky_painting.png` (convert a JPEG to PNG first; the file is copied as it is) and converted on this and every later run, opaque. The wrap works as for the moon's sky but over a wider overlap (320 columns, fine detail across 128), because generated paintings rarely meet at their ends. There is no flat zenith cap: the cloth hangs from a dark crown and its folds should converge overhead like the peak of a canopy, so the painting's own top is kept and its rows are only softened around the circle as much as the projection squeezes them. Delete the source file to go back to the procedural silk.

Layout the game expects:

- Upper-hemisphere equirectangular, 2:1. The top edge is the zenith and the bottom edge is the horizon.
- The dome is centred 240 blocks under the ground, so from where a player stands the bottom fifth of the image is never seen. Keep the interest in the upper four fifths.
- The game multiplies the painting by its own colour: deep oxblood at night, vermilion by day. Paint it mid-valued, like cloth at dusk, so both work. Do not paint it very dark.
- The tear, its burning edges, the stitches, the needle, the hand's shadow and the star band are all drawn in game. The painted cloth must be whole.

## Silent-daylight sky (无声白昼)

The pale sky that covers the ordinary one during 无声白昼 is [hush_sky.png](../../src/main/resources/assets/veilborne/textures/environment/hush_sky.png). It is now generated from the [painted source](../../tools/art/source/hush_sky_painting.png). The painting was made with an image generation model on 2026-09-27 from the prompt below, and supplied by the user as a 1568×784 JPEG (warm ivory greys, saturation at most 0.09, no bright disc). It was converted losslessly to PNG and installed through `--hush-sky`; no external reference image is known to have been used. Its ends did not meet exactly (the raw step across the join was about 4.5 times an ordinary column-to-column step); after the converter's cross-fade the step equals an ordinary one (0.0010 against 0.0008). Because the conversion leaves it very low in contrast, `hush_sky.fsh` weights it at 0.42 + 0.75 × paint (mean unchanged), enough for its held clouds to read behind the frozen blocky ones. `sky()` in `tools/art/hush.py` remains as the procedural fallback when the source file is absent: a bleached noon that has stopped, with still stratus banks along the horizon, a few thin tilted cirrus strokes low over them, soft held heaps of cloud, a calm blank zenith and a faint paper grain.

To install another painting, run `python tools/generate_assets.py --no-sound --hush-sky path/to/painting.png` (convert a JPEG to PNG first; the file is copied as it is). The file is copied to `tools/art/source/hush_sky_painting.png` and converted on this and every later run. The ends are wrapped and the zenith softened as for the scarlet seam's cloth (`thread.painted_sky`). Then it is drained to about 12% of its colour and its brightness is re-centred on the grey the shader expects (mean 0.72, contrast × 0.8), so a painting of any exposure fits. Delete the source file to go back to the procedural stand-in.

Layout the game expects:

- Upper-hemisphere equirectangular, 2:1. The top edge is the zenith and the bottom edge is the horizon.
- The texture is fixed to the world's directions and never moves: the clouds in it are held still. Keep the interest in the lower half, near the horizon, and the upper half calm and almost blank.
- The shader draws the paper-white sun or moon, the black sun, the lens that bends the sky round it, the black corona, the colour ribbons and the white line on the horizon. The painting must have none of them.
- Anything that runs along the sky at one height becomes a circle round the zenith when you look up. Avoid rings, evenly spaced bands and long horizontal streaks high in the sky.

## Verdant-dream canopy (苍翠梦潮)

The deep forest overhead during 苍翠梦潮 is [dream_sky.png](../../src/main/resources/assets/veilborne/textures/environment/dream_sky.png). It is the far layer of the canopy only: `dream_sky.fsh` projects it onto a dome anchored over the event (as it was painted, from the event's centre, so everyone sees the same leaves) and draws the near forest over it procedurally: the crowns of the nearest trees seen from beneath (overlapping, ragged at the edges, lit from behind, with gaps between them), their trunks and forking branches, the growing front, the honey-gold turn and the leaf fall, fireflies, the lamps of little hanging houses, and the real sun or moon where the leaves have gone. The hanging crowns, roots and the dream tree are meshes (`DreamGrove`), the deer is rebuilt every frame (`DeerModel`); none of them come from the painting.

No painting has been supplied yet, so the file is generated by `sky()` in `tools/art/dream.py`: the underside of a forest drawn on the ceiling's own plane (so its perspective is right and nothing rings the zenith) as warped-noise foliage, dark in its depths and lit green-gold at its thin edges, a deeper layer of leaves through most gaps and pale sunlit sky through a few; toward the horizon, hanging crowns in silhouette with aerial roots trailing below them, fading into jade haze; a few lamps deep in the leaves.

It is copied to `tools/art/source/dream_sky_painting.png` and converted on this and every later run: the ends are wrapped and the zenith softened as for the scarlet seam's cloth (`thread.painted_sky`), then its exposure is settled to a mean luminance of 0.4 so the shader's lighting sits on it as it does on the stand-in. Layout the game expects:

- It is the deep forest seen from far below, behind the near crowns: most of it will show only through the gaps between them, so what matters is its colour, its light and its depth, not fine detail.

- Anything that runs along the sky at one height becomes a circle round the zenith when you look up. Avoid rings, evenly spaced bands and long horizontal streaks high in the sky.
